PIANO & ENGLISH

Valeria

With over 10 years of teaching experience and a Bachelor of Music with a focus on music pedagogy and piano teaching, Valeria has taught students of all ages and levels — from beginners discovering the piano for the first time to advanced students developing confident and expressive performance.

Her approach is personal and encouraging: understanding each student's goals, strengths and challenges, and finding the right way to help them progress. She believes mistakes are a natural and valuable part of learning — an opportunity to understand, improve and grow.

Valeria also teaches English from Italian, French, German and Spanish. She speaks six languages fluently and is currently learning a seventh, giving her first-hand insight into the hurdles that come with learning a new language and the confidence it takes to actually use it.

Lessons are practical, supportive and adapted to each student's needs, whether the goal is everyday conversation, study, work, musical development or performance.

CHESS

Richard

Richard has been passionate about chess since childhood. Largely self-taught in his early years, he has since competed in tournaments and developed a strong understanding of the game through years of playing and practice.

His teaching is practical, supportive and adapted to each student's level. Rather than simply memorising moves, students learn how to think, make decisions and understand the ideas behind the game.

Lessons can include analysing games, exploring key concepts such as tactics and openings, and putting new skills into practice through play.

OUR APPROACH

Your goals. Your pace.
Your way of learning.

At Mind & Muse, we believe good teaching starts with understanding the person behind the student. Everyone learns differently, so lessons are shaped around individual goals, experience, strengths and challenges.

We want lessons to be a place where you can ask questions, try new things and challenge yourself. Mistakes are part of learning. They are not something to be afraid of — they show us where there is room to grow, help us understand what needs more attention, and give us an opportunity to improve.

The goal is not simply to teach techniques or information, but to help you understand what you're doing, build confidence and gradually become more independent in your learning.

With the right guidance, purposeful practice and patience, small improvements can grow into meaningful progress.

“The goal isn't simply to learn something new —
it's to feel confident using it.”
